Bias and variance in one dimension

\[\begin{aligned} \text{Model:}\quad & Y_i=\theta^*x_i+\varepsilon_i,\\ & \mathbb E[\varepsilon_i]=0,\ \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i)=\sigma^2,\text{ independent}\\[6pt] \htmlClass{u2}{\text{OLS:}\quad} & \htmlClass{u2}{\widehat\theta=\frac{\sum_i x_iY_i}{\sum_i x_i^2}}\\[6pt] \htmlClass{u3}{\text{Bias:}\quad} & \htmlClass{u3}{\mathbb E[\widehat\theta]-\theta^*=\frac{\sum_i x_i\,\mathbb E[Y_i]}{\sum_i x_i^2}-\theta^*}\\ & \htmlClass{u3}{\phantom{\mathbb E[\widehat\theta]-\theta^*}=\frac{\sum_i x_i\,\theta^*x_i}{\sum_i x_i^2}-\theta^*=0}\\[6pt] \htmlClass{u4}{\text{Variance:}\quad} & \htmlClass{u4}{\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta)=\frac{\sum_i x_i^2\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)}{\big(\sum_i x_i^2\big)^2}=\frac{\sigma^2}{\sum_i x_i^2}} \end{aligned}\]

$\theta^*=1$, $\sigma=0.5$; thin lines: fits to five more samples (numbers are illustrative)

The general regression model specifies two moments

\[\bY=X\btheta^*+\boldsymbol\varepsilon,\qquad\E[\boldsymbol\varepsilon]=\boldsymbol 0,\qquad\cov(\boldsymbol\varepsilon)=\sigma^2 I_n.\]
\[\E[\bY\mid X]=X\btheta^*,\qquad\cov(\bY\mid X)=\sigma^2 I_n.\] The errors have mean zero, common variance, and zero covariance across observations.

Maximum likelihood reveals least squares

Under independent, constant-variance Gaussian noise: $\boldsymbol\varepsilon\sim\cN(\mathbf0,\sigma^2I_n)$.

\[\begin{aligned}\ell(\btheta,\sigma^2)&=\log p(\by\mid X,\btheta,\sigma^2)\\&=-\frac n2\log(2\pi\sigma^2)-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\btheta^\top\bx_i)^2.\end{aligned}\]
