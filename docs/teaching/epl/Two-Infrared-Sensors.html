Fisher information

The relative entropy between the distributions at two temperatures measures how easily readings at one can be told apart from readings at the other. For a sensor with gain g and noise σ, raising the temperature by δ gives

D(pθ‖pθ+δ)=g2δ22σ2.

The relative entropy grows with the square of δ, faster for the less noisy sensor. Its curvature at δ = 0 is the Fisher information.

Definition

ℐ(θ)=limδ→02δ2D(pθ‖pθ+δ)

equivalently

D(pθ‖pθ+δ)=12ℐ(θ)δ2+o(δ2)

For both sensors the relative entropy is exactly quadratic, so ℐ(θ) = g2/σ2 at every temperature: for sensor A and for sensor B. Sensor A's Fisher information is times sensor B's, and so is the relative entropy produced by any change in temperature.

Relative entropies of independent readings add, so 20 readings carry 20 times the Fisher information of one: for sensor A and for sensor B.

Both sensors have gain g = 1 mV per °C and read zero when the object is at their own temperature, 25 °C. A reading is v = g(θ − 25) + ε with ε ~ N(0, σ2), so p(v; θ) = N(v; g(θ − 25), σ2). Its Fisher information is ℐ(θ) = g2/σ2, and D(pθ₀ ‖ pθ) = ℐ(θ)(θ − θ0)2/2 exactly.
